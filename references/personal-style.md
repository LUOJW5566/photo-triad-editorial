# Personal Style: Photo Triad Editorial

This guide translates the observed direction of the user's `Hong Kong City Beats` work into reusable decisions. It is a style guide, not a source of subjects or a license to reproduce a particular composition.

## Visual thesis

Begin with the credibility of a real photograph, then move into a designed visual memory. The result should feel like a page from a contemporary city image journal: observant, cinematic in its hierarchy, but restrained in its abstraction. The photograph carries evidence; the lower construction carries interpretation.

## Signature tendencies

- Prefer a strong vertical editorial field with a clear upper/lower relationship.
- Let one source-specific gesture dominate: a traffic flow, building rhythm, diagonal shadow, human cluster, roofline, reflection, or color interruption.
- Use asymmetry, measured pauses, and negative space rather than filling every region.
- Favor dark structural neutrals with one or two source-derived color notes. A saturated red, amber, green, or blue may act as a small signal only when the photograph already contains it.
- Keep the mood observational and urban-aware even when the source is natural. Do not inflate a quiet scene into fantasy, spectacle, or advertising drama.
- Make the lower abstraction feel designed and tactile through proportion, overlap, and edge decisions—not through random grain or decorative aging.

## What to borrow from the reference work

Borrow the editorial hierarchy, source-aware color drama, compact narrative feeling, and deliberate balance between image weight and open field. Translate the former poster-like energy into geometry, volume, and pixel structure when the user asks for the triad fusion. Keep typography nearly silent: date and theme only.

## What not to copy

Do not reuse the reference image's city, taxi, signs, people, words, title treatment, exact layout, or specific palette. Do not imitate a commercial magazine template. Every object and color in a new work must be justified by the current uploaded photograph.

## Surface and restraint

The default panel is clean and spacious. Use matte marks, controlled edges, and sparse volume. Do not add faux paper stains, yellow cast, distressed borders, glow, lens flare, smoke, or a generalized vintage filter. If a future request explicitly asks for print texture, keep it localized, low-contrast, and subordinate to the source relationships.
